Therapeutic PETase Design: PANTS mines metagenomic sequence space for PET-degrading enzymes to enable protein engineering and therapeutic discovery.

PET is the plastic in drinks bottles and polyester clothing, and fragments of it now turn up in human blood and tissue. A few bacterial enzymes can cut PET apart, but almost every one was found or engineered for an industrial recycling reactor: hot, alkaline, nothing like a human body. A therapeutic enzyme has to work at 37 °C in blood, and the published lineage has been moving steadily the other way.

So PANTS looks somewhere else. Metagenomics reads the DNA of whole microbial communities straight from the environment, without culturing anything, reaching the vast majority of bacteria nobody has ever grown in a lab. PANTS searches four communities with decades of plastic exposure and every reason to have evolved a use for it: the human gut, compost, the ocean surface where debris collects, and landfill. Some 15 million protein sequences are scanned for the polyesterase fold, then filtered on whether the three catalytic residues actually meet in space rather than merely appearing in the sequence. Survivors get a predicted structure, an active-site measurement and a rank.

The honest part. Finding this enzyme family is easy. Telling which of its members actually chew through PET is not, and that is the open problem PANTS exists to work on. Only {{ counts.evidenced }} of the {{ counts.positives }} enzymes it learns from rest on a published measurement; the rest carry a label a computer inferred from sequence similarity. A model trained on those inferred labels looks flawless, but only because it has rediscovered the rule that made them. Scores here rank candidates worth testing at the bench. They are not evidence that any candidate works. Read the Methods page before using PANTS.

Known and characterised enzymes

Every named PETase and polyesterase in the reference set, grouped by lineage with each wild type heading the variants built on it. Read down the Topt column within a lineage and the pattern is the one that motivates this project: engineering runs away from 37 °C, the therapeutic target, which is marked on every row at or below it. Each row opens a page carrying its structure, active-site geometry, mutation set, measured activity and every external identifier.
