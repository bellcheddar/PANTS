Methods and limitations

Spec section 7 calls this page non-negotiable "given how easily this kind of tool is over-read". This project has more to disclose than most.

The headline caveat. A classifier trained on this catalogue's own labels scores AUC 1.000 against hard negatives. That is a failure, not a success: {{ counts.positives - counts.evidenced }} of {{ counts.positives }} positives were labelled by sequence similarity (ECO:0000256), so a sequence-embedding model reproducing them is close to tautological. Only {{ counts.evidenced }} positives carry experimental evidence, and they all fall in a single cluster at 30% identity, so a head trained on measured activity alone cannot currently be evaluated by cluster-split cross-validation at all.

What this catalogue therefore is: a retrieval ranking over triad-complete metagenomic candidates, with annotation-independent geometry measured on predicted structures. What it is not: a demonstrated model of PET-hydrolysing activity.

Limitations

  1. Positives number in the low hundreds and most carry family annotation rather than measured PET activity.
  2. Published activity data is not harmonised across assay formats. Absolute rate predictions should not be trusted.
  3. Crystalline PET degradation at 37 °C by any known enzyme is slow. PANTS ranks relative promise, not therapeutic viability.
  4. Predicted structures are predictions. Cleft geometry on a metagenomic sequence with no close homologue carries real uncertainty, and a brittle version of that measurement scored IsPETase's own prediction at half its crystal value before being fixed.
  5. Nothing here addresses delivery, immunogenicity, biodistribution, or what happens to liberated TPA and EG in vivo.
  6. Metagenomic candidates may come from unculturable organisms, may not express in a standard host, and may be fragments or misassemblies.
  7. Cleft width currently separates a fungal cutinase from the polyesterase family, but does not order that family by PET activity. Family-level separation is a much easier task than the one that matters.
